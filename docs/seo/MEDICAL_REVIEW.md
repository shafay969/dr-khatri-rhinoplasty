# Medical content and evidence review

These rules apply to Dr Vijay Khatri’s professional identity, treatment pages, educational articles, FAQs, patient proof and structured data. They describe review procedures; detailed findings, approvals and sensitive evidence are kept privately.

## Publication gates

- Confirm professional facts against appropriate evidence: registered name/specialty, credential titles, training, current affiliations and availability. Use `src/data/business.ts` as prior client-confirmed provenance; do not infer that all surrounding narrative has the same status.
- Require surgeon approval before publishing substantive new or changed clinical claims, including indications, outcomes, risks, recovery, pain, candidacy, pre-/post-operative instructions and comparisons. Record the exact reviewed version, reviewer, date and scope privately. Do not add a “medically reviewed” credit before review occurs.
- Confirm each testimonial/photo case is authentic, correctly labelled and permitted for the intended use. Public availability and the presence of an asset file do not establish consent. Store confirmation and access references privately; do not commit signed forms, identifiable medical records or patient images obtained from private sources.
- Do not present demonstration material as patient evidence or keep unsupported ratings, procedure counts or satisfaction figures as marketing proof. Do not replace a known unsupported assertion with a newly invented claim.
- Avoid unsupported superiority or absolute promises, including best-in-country, guaranteed outcomes, no tissue injury, no risk, painless surgery or universal recovery timelines. Search-query research containing “best” does not authorize that advertising claim.

A source cleanup can be staged and reviewed without production permission. Production publishing remains separately gated even when a change removes an unsafe assertion. No substantive replacement clinical copy should be published without appropriate approval.

## Structured data

Represent accurate visible facts only. Distinguish the person, any actual practice and the host hospital. Do not imply a visiting consultant owns the hospital, invent office branches or fill missing fields to satisfy a tool. Add reviews/ratings, credential claims, hours and prices only when substantiated and appropriate to the schema type.

Check JSON syntax, relevant Schema.org vocabulary and currently supported Google features separately. Validation does not guarantee rich results or a ranking effect. Google retired FAQ rich results in 2026; FAQPage can still carry semantics for visible questions but must not be sold as an FAQ search appearance benefit. [Official Google updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), [structured-data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies).

## Local listings and reviews

Verify an existing profile and individual-practitioner eligibility before creating or editing a listing. Use accurate name, specialty, direct contact details, actual location and confirmed availability; never create a separate profile for every technique. An observed public listing does not establish management access. Public edits require approval. [Google practitioner rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

Do not buy reviews, select only satisfied patients, offer incentives or pressure users to add keywords. Do not contact patients without authorization. A review reply must not confirm a treatment relationship or disclose health information. [Google Maps policies](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114).

## Privacy and regulatory checks

Never send patient names, contact details, diagnoses, symptoms, photographs, appointment contents or sensitive treatment data to analytics or reporting systems. Inspect URLs, query parameters, referrers and third-party scripts before approving any minimal generic conversion event. No session recording, remarketing pixels or sensitive tracking without explicit authorization and an appropriate review.

Verify current PMDC advertising/social-media requirements and applicable local privacy obligations from authoritative sources before claiming compliance. PMDC announced a revised Code of Ethics in 2026, so older copies alone do not establish the current rules. Escalate unresolved legal/clinical interpretation to the appropriate qualified reviewer. [Official PMDC release](https://pmdc.pk/Documents/press/PM%26DC%20REINFORCES%20ETHICS%20AND%20PATIENT%20SAFETY%20WITH%20LANDMARK%20REFORMS.pdf).
